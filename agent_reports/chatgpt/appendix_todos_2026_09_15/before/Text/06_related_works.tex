\paragraph{Planning and look-ahead in LMs.} Prior work shows that autoregressive LMs encode information about future tokens \citep{pal2023futurelens} and plan toward constraints in later output \citep{lindsey2025biology}. We study the upcoming computation at the level of an instrumental sub-goal. Our experiments ask whether its representation can be read before implementation and transferred to guide the same operation in a new context.

\paragraph{Portable task representations and function abstractions.} Function vectors are compact representations of demonstrated input--output tasks, carried by a small set of attention heads and transferable to new contexts \citep{todd2024function}. Concept-induction heads carry word representations that mediate translation across languages \citep{feucht2025dual}. Filter heads encode predicates in their query states and reapply them across collections, formats, languages, and tasks \citep{sensharma2025filterheads}. Our work examines a related abstraction during multi-step generation: the particular sub-goal the model is about to implement. We localize its encoding in head contributions, verbalize its content, and test its portability across programming languages.

\paragraph{Verbalizing internal representations.} Patchscope uses a language model to express information extracted from its hidden states \citep{ghandeharioun2024patchscope}. \goalscope applies this approach to the contributions of causally localized attention heads at a single source position. The cached signal is reused throughout probe generation to obtain a multi-token description or code implementation. OVReader extends this readout to intermediate residual states using the selected heads' value and output projections.

\paragraph{High-level interpretability of goal-directed behavior.} The high-level interpretability agenda studies model behavior through abstractions that can be investigated without a complete account of every underlying computation \citep{greenblatt2024highlevel}. We use an instrumental sub-goal as such an abstraction. Cross-language transfer tests whether its encoding can guide different implementations, while restrictions on goal-head attention test its role in the model's own generation.
