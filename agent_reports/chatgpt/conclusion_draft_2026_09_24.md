# Conclusion

We find that LLMs encode abstract sub-goals before acting on them. In multi-step coding tasks, the outputs of a sparse set of attention heads expose the upcoming operation: the encoding can be verbalized in natural language and transplanted to trigger the same operation in another programming language. These heads also participate in the model's native execution. Preserving their access to task information largely preserves implementation, while restricting a broader set sharply impairs it. Together, these findings connect what the model is about to do with an internal representation that can guide different realizations of that operation.

The temporal analysis shows that sub-goal content remains recognizable across the tokens of its implementation. We propose repeated recomputation from the task and progress as an account of this functional persistence. Query and key--value transfers identify information that influences which operation is selected, while leaving open where and how the proposed DIFF computation occurs. Temporal continuity also coexists with weak recovery from misleading implementation prefixes: representing an operation does not guarantee that the model can sustain its pursuit under disruption.

Readouts across model families and in non-code settings suggest that these tools extend beyond the controlled coding setup, although their reliability varies. GoalScope provides a way to inspect sub-goal content as generation proceeds and to test its influence through intervention. An important next step is to connect how an operation is selected, sustained during execution, and retired upon completion. Applying the same readouts during failures could reveal whether the model loses track of its sub-goal or retains it but fails to carry it out.
